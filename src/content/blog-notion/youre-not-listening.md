<h2 id="the-book-in-3-sentences">The Book in 3 Sentences</h2>

<ol class="nlist"><li>Listening goes beyond just hearing what people say - it&#x27;s about paying attention to how they say it, what they do while they are saying it, what the context/topic of conversation is, their current emotions/inner dialogue, their individual background (native language, culture, familial upbringing), how what they say resonates within you (i.e. using conversational sensitivity to pick up on other physical, chemical, emotional, and intellectual cues), and literally which ear the information is coming into.<details><summary>More on the lateralization on hearing</summary><div class="dtl"><figure><img src="/images/blog/youre-not-listening-57.webp" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure></div></details></li><li>The inability to listen properly will hinder your personal growth and limit the depth of relationships that you can have with the people around you; your ability to be a good listener is directly correlated to the depth of relationships you build.</li><li>The best thing you could possibly do as a listener is remember that it has NOTHING to do with you, it is all about them. Be genenuinly curious about what they are saying and aim to learn as much as you can.<details><summary>Some actionable do&#x27;s and don&#x27;ts to be a better listener</summary><div class="dtl"><div class="callout bg-gray-light"><div class="callout-icon">✅</div><div class="callout-body"><p>Do&#x27;s:</p><ul class="nlist"><li>Be more curious</li><li>Ask relevant questions</li><li>Ask for clarification if you did not fully understand something</li><li>Think of listening as being similar to meditation. You want to rid your brain of all other thoughts - let yourself get fully immersed in the speakers mind</li><li>As a more general cue, try to follow the philosopher <span class="tc-blue"><strong><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_principle#Grice&#x27;s_maxims">Paul Grice&#x27;s four maxims of conversations</a></strong></span> to assess the effectiveness of your dialogue</li></ul></div></div><div class="callout bg-gray-light"><div class="callout-icon">🚫</div><div class="callout-body"><p>Don&#x27;ts:</p><ul class="nlist"><li>Interrupt or try to finish their sentences. Don&#x27;t assume you know what they are going to say</li><li>Minimize their concerns, or try to &#x27;solve&#x27; their problems</li><li>Respond vaguely or illogically to what was just said</li><li>Look at anything other than the speaker. This is why I think that recording conversations for later analysis/review is extremely useful!</li><li>Fidget (e.g., tapping on the table, frequently shifting positions, clicking a pen, etc.)</li><li>Redirect the conversation to something that you are narrowly experienced in - it&#x27;s ok to let them lead</li></ul></div></div></div></details></li></ol>

<h2 id="impressions">Impressions 🤔</h2>

Although I read this book sometime last year, my takeaways in the 'How this Book Changed Me' below have still stuck with me ever since, and I'm confident that I will use these learnings for the rest of my life.

I did find that as I progressed through the book, the message became somewhat repetitive and there were generally less captivating real-world examples or stories to follow along with - in the final chapters, I found myself focusing less on the general content and more on 'quotable lines' or new terminologies.

<h2 id="who-should-read-it">Who Should Read It❓</h2>

I chose to read this book after it being recommended by one of my coworkers while I was working in a retail position at an Apple Store. Since then, my learnings have translated into all of my professional experiences and personal relationships.

I would recommend this book to anyone is looking to level up their personal relationships and learn how to have better interactions with other people in general.

More specifically, if you are a leader of any sort (i.e., executive, manager, team leader, parent, captain, etc.), the value that you can bring to your team by becoming a better listener is exponential.
